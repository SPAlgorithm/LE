Ladhe's Quad Conjecture: A New Pattern in Prime Quadruplets

DESCRIPTION
A brand new pattern in the prime numbers. Every prime quadruplet (p, p+2, p+6, p+8), the tightest family of four primes that exists, can be written as the largest member of the previous quadruplet plus a few distinct members of earlier quadruplets, for example 13001 = 9439 + 3461 + 101. Seeded by the "royal quad" 2, 3, 5, 7, the chain runs through every prime quadruplet below one billion, all 28,387 of them, with no exception. This video explains the conjecture, the exceptions, what it does and does not say about finding the next quadruplet, a proved companion theorem (every integer below the next quadruplet is a sum of chain members plus one small expression in 2, 3, 5, 7), the additive, multiplicative and exponential ways of writing each member, and open questions.

Invented by Shubham Ladhe, Saloni Ladhe and Pankaj Ladhe.

Companion iOS app (LE-Games, free): https://apps.apple.com/app/id6767880385
Paper, tool (LC) and the full dataset to 10^9: https://github.com/SPAlgorithm/LE (folder LC)
